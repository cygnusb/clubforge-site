Privacy helpers for clubs

Building blocks for clubs that run clubforge on their own server: for the club app’s privacy notice, for the record of processing activities, and a plain overview of the deletion periods.

Not legal advice, a template to adapt. The texts describe what the clubforge software does. What your club does on top of that (which host, which mail provider, which features are on) you have to add and check yourselves. The controller under the GDPR is the club. If it has a data protection officer, they belong in this.

What is already there

  • A privacy page in the app. The server serves a neutral standard text at /datenschutz, with the controller, purposes, legal bases, recipients, periods, and rights. The app links it in the profile and on the login page. The person running the server enters the club’s contact and address; the club fills in anything in square brackets (such as the host). Or the page can forward to a privacy notice of the club’s own.
  • A “delete account and data” page at /konto-loeschen, public and without signing in.
  • Access: each member downloads their own data in the app (Profile › Download my data).

The building blocks below help when you write your own privacy notice or complete the standard page.

Building blocks for the privacy notice

Replace the text in square brackets. Leave out building blocks for features you do not use.

Basis

Scope. These notes apply to the app and the web app of [club name] at [app.your-club.example]. They add to the club’s privacy information about membership.

Controller is [club name, address]. Contact for privacy questions: [address].

Operation. The app runs on a server the club operates with [hosting provider] in [location, e.g. Germany]. There is a data processing agreement with the provider. The clubforge software is open source; its developers have no access to the club’s data.

No tracking. The app contains no advertising and no analytics or tracking services.

Who sees the data

  • Members see what belongs to their groups, events, and conversations. Optional profile details are seen only by whoever the person picks for each field (nobody, their own group, or everyone).
  • Admins of the club or of a group, as far as their task requires, for example when reviewing reported content.
  • The hosting provider [name] as a processor.
  • For push notifications, the services of Apple, Google, or Mozilla receive a device address and a short text.
  • The club sends email through [email provider].

Messages are not end-to-end encrypted. They are encrypted in transit and stored on the club’s server.

Building blocks for features you have turned on

Photos, files, and tracks. Attachments sit in the club’s storage at [storage provider, location]; there is a data processing agreement with the provider. The server removes place, time, and device details from photos, and timestamps, heart rate, and similar sensor data from GPX files. An attachment is kept as long as the post it belongs to[; deleted files stay in storage for up to [lifecycle rule period]].

Maps. Maps come from the club’s server; no outside map service learns who looks at which track or place. Only “plan a route” passes the destination’s coordinates to the device’s maps app.

Self-deleting messages. Messages with an expiry are deleted on the server at most one hour after they expire. They remain in the encrypted backups for up to 30 days longer.

Membership system. The app takes name, year joined, status, email addresses, and sections from [easyVerein / the club’s membership system]. These details cannot be changed in the app; corrections go through the membership system.

Calendar subscription. Whoever subscribes to their events gets a personal link that their own calendar (for example from Apple, Google, or Microsoft) fetches regularly. A new link can be created in the app at any time; the old one then stops working.

The club’s Google Calendar. Events meant for the whole club or for open groups are copied by the app into the club’s calendars in Google Workspace: title, time, place, a shortened description, and a link. Who said yes, comments, guests, rides, messages, and other details about people are never copied. [If the calendar is public or embedded on the club website, these events are public.]

Integrations. Tools of the club, such as [hall schedule], read events through an interface and, of members, only the profile details released to all members, and show their own data in the app. They do not see private groups. They are run by [club or contractor].

How long, and your rights

How long. After someone leaves, access is blocked at once and the person is anonymized after 30 days. Messages stay without a name, and without content if that was requested. Further periods: the bell 90 days, deleted messages 30 days, responses 24 months after the event (only counts after that), guest names 3 months after the event, reports 12 months after they are closed, the admin log 2 years, sessions 90 days after they end, backups 30 days.

Your rights. Access and portability: in the app under Profile › Download my data. Correction: profile details yourself in the app, name and address through the membership system. Erasure: in the app under Profile › Delete account and data, or with the club. Withdrawal: releases and notifications at any time in the profile. A complaint to a data protection authority, for example the one of the state where the club has its seat.

Still without a fixed period is the server’s internal log of events and commands. That is also listed in the known limitations of the current version; mention it in your notes until then.

A deletion request, step by step: a member files it in the app, and the club admins are told. They remove the person in the membership system and mark the request done; access ends with the next sync, and 30 days later the server anonymizes on its own.
